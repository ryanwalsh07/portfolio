orbit A heads-up display app for neurodiverse drivers

01The problem

Infotainment systems are built for the average driver.

Car manufacturers compete on the driving experience, and infotainment systems are a big part of that. They’re practical, but they’re also distracting, especially for neurodiverse drivers, because they offer little flexibility in how information is shown while on the move.

The brief

A user-centred concept that helps neurodiverse drivers see and follow directions within their peripheral view, without getting distracted on the way to their destination.

What it needed to do

  • Show directions without pulling attention off the road
  • Offer flexible accessibility options on the go
  • Be shaped by what neurodiverse drivers say they need
  • Feel simple to set up and easy to trust

02Why it matters

Distraction is a road safety problem

2,563

accidents in 2019 were caused by distractions in the vehicle, and 63 of them were fatal.1

73%

of the drivers I surveyed struggle to read directions.2

92%

said they would benefit from using a HUD.2

Every 16 minutes, someone is killed or seriously injured on UK roads. Something needs to change.

Sources: 1. UK road safety statistics for 2019, as quoted in my research blog. 2. My own survey of drivers of all ages.

04Designing for neurodiversity

Flexible by default, so drivers can make it their own

Wireframes made the idea tangible and showed the details a driver would want in everyday use. From there I refined the onboarding, the settings and the visual language over two semesters.

The brand and interface were shaped for neurodiverse users from the start, including clear type, considered scale and options for colourblind drivers.

05Testing

Testing it remotely, then refining

In the second semester I ran a remote usability testing phase, then gathered the results and feedback to shape the next round of changes. I also took the concept to industry reviewers and co-founder sprints, using their feedback to decide where orbit should go next.

A concept for neurodiverse drivers is only useful if it works for them, so each round of feedback changed the design.
